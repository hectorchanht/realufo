## 3. /cases/ — Cold Cases · stance: analyst

**Title:** Tremonton 1952: Navy analysts said 3,780 mph light sources. Blue Book wanted an ornithologist.

**Body:**

Same film, two very different readings, both now declassified:

The film itself: 16mm Kodachrome, shot handheld near Tremonton, Utah on 2 July 1952 by a Navy Chief Warrant Officer.
https://realufo.org/doc/DOW-UAP-PR159

Project Blue Book's disposition form (15 Oct 1952) suggests an ornithologist review the film to see if the objects were birds. It also notes no sound and no exhaust trails.
https://realufo.org/doc/DOW-UAP-D102

The Naval Photographic Interpretation Center's analysis (May 1953) concluded the objects were light sources that didn't match known natural or man-made objects, and calculated a velocity of 3,780 mph. The report flags its own assumptions as uncertain.
https://realufo.org/doc/DOW-UAP-D098

Birds catching the sun, or something the Navy's own photo lab couldn't explain? Which document do you trust more, and why?

## 5. /skeptic/ — Debunk · stance: skeptic

**Title:** Colorado Springs: 5 soldiers saw an object near Cheyenne Mountain. The analysis says snow glare. Convinced?

**Body:**

Five U.S. Army service members reported a stationary, angular object near Cheyenne Mountain on 15 Feb 2022 that disappeared suddenly. The analysis suggests sunlight backscattered off snow, lighting up low clouds. It rates that assessment **low confidence** and notes no aircraft or balloons were active at the time.
https://realufo.org/doc/ICA-UAP-D001

Colorado shows up more than once in the files: an FBI interview about a silent "dark translucent triangle" flying low over a house in October 2023…
https://realufo.org/doc/FBI-UAP-D026

…and, elsewhere in Colorado, an officer's 15-minute phone video of a light with "red, blue, and green flashing lights," also October 2023.
https://realufo.org/doc/LLE-UAP-PR002

Make the prosaic case. Does snow backscatter fit what the soldiers described? What would you need to see to rule it out?
