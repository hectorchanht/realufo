## Archive hits (quote only these in the story)

- **255413270UFOsandDefenseWhatShouldwePrepareFor** (French COMETA report "UFOs and Defense: What Should We Prepare For?", 1999), Appendix on disinformation:
  - p.74: "The point of departure is the Bennewicz case" (translation spells it Bennewicz). He "recorded pulsed microwaves from a testing ground at Kirtland (New Mexico) Air Force base" and blamed UFOs controlling "abductees" with implants.
  - p.74: AFOSI "and, namely, its special agent Doty" … "induced him to make fantastic 'revelations'": kidnappings, implants, joint US–"EBE" bases in New Mexico and Nevada.

  - p.40: effects "being studied at the Air Force Weapons Laboratory at Kirtland AFB".

### Key people
- Paul Bennewitz: Albuquerque physicist/businessman; the legend's first believer.
- Richard Doty: AFOSI agent at Kirtland; admits spreading disinformation; admissions are themselves disputed.
- William Moore: ufologist who helped feed Bennewitz; confessed 1989.
- Greg Bishop: author of *Project Beta*; thinks a Dulce base "extremely unlikely."
- Phil Schneider: 1990s lecturer; Wikipedia says he borrowed from Lear.
- John Lear: 1987 "confirmation" of the base.
- Thomas Castello: alleged Dulce security man; no evidence he existed.
- Kenneth Rommel: ex-FBI investigator, 1980 mutilation report.
- Gabe Valdez: state officer; blamed government, not aliens (son Greg says he "never believed there was alien activity").

### Juiciest angles
1. The legend was built by the Air Force itself: the "cover-up" was real, but the cover was alien stories hiding mundane secrets.
2. Same Kirtland memo, two readings: AFOSI treated Bennewitz as a security nuisance on 28 Oct 1980; he saw proof of aliens. Show the form (use our archive's copy).
3. The mutilation probe: FBI had jurisdiction only because Dulce is on Indian land; the Bureau's verdict was predators.
4. There really was a big hole under the area: Gasbuggy (1967, nuclear). Valdez's son ties mutilations to it; unproven.
5. Hoax pile-up: Aquarius teletype, Dulce Papers, 6th Day photo, Schneider. Each layer borrows the last: AARO's "circular reporting" in miniature.
